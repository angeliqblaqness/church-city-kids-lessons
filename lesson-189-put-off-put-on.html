Put Off, Put On

While Paul sat chained in Rome, he wrote letters to churches far away. He told them something you do every morning: change your clothes. Take off the old self. Put on the new.

MEMORY VERSE · Colossians 2:6–7
Therefore, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him, rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught, abounding in thanksgiving.

▶ Watch first · the story≈ 1 min 50
Before the film: point to Paul's Journeys Map. "We've been following Paul to Rome. How was this trip different from the first three?" (He was a prisoner.) "What caused the shipwreck?" (A terrible storm.) "What did Paul do with his visitors?" (Told them the gospel.)

1Put off the old self

Ever played outside until you were so sweaty and muddy you couldn't wait to change? Paul says that's how a Christian should feel about sin. Get the dirty stuff off. But that's only half of it: you have to put something clean on.

▶ Watch · Ephesians 4:20–24then try it yourself
EPHESIANS 4:22–24
…to put off your old self, which belongs to your former manner of life and is corrupt through deceitful desires, and to be renewed in the spirit of your minds, and to put on the new self, created after the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness.
CLASS NOTES · ROW 1
Put off the old self. It is C _ R R _ P T (spoiled, rotten). Put on the new self. It is righteous and H _ L Y.
Answers: corrupt · holy. "Where does Paul say truth is found?" (In Jesus, v. 21.) "What is the key to changing?" (Renewed in the spirit of your minds, v. 23.) Have students underline those phrases on their Scripture Sheet.

2Set your mind above

Paul wrote the same idea to the Colossians, and he told them where to point their minds.

▶ Watch · Colossians 3:1–2then try it yourself
COLOSSIANS 3:1–2
If then you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God. Set your minds on things that are above, not on things that are on earth.
CLASS NOTES · ROW 2
To put off the old self, don't set your mind on things on E _ R T H. To put on the new self, set your mind on things A B _ V E.
Answers: earth · above. "Things on earth" = sins like greed, selfish desires, impurity. Ask: "Does becoming a child of God mean you can put off everything sinful right away?" Allow discussion; the new self is being renewed: a process.

3What to take off

Paul doesn't leave it vague. He names six muddy things.

▶ Watch · Colossians 3:8–10then fill in the vowels
COLOSSIANS 3:8–10
But now you must put them all away: anger, wrath, malice, slander, and obscene talk from your mouth. Do not lie to one another, seeing that you have put off the old self with its practices and have put on the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge after the image of its creator.

Class Notes · PUT OFF column · add the missing vowels

Tap a word, then tap the vowels in order. A · E · I · O · U.

Define as you go: anger and wrath are losing your temper; malice = hatred; slander = saying false things about someone; obscene talk = dirty or inappropriate words; then lying. Ask which phrases match Ephesians (put off the old self, put on the new self, renewed in knowledge/mind).

4What to put on

Ever had a habit your parents wanted you to quit? Biting your nails, chewing with your mouth open, saying "like" every three words? If all you think about is not doing it, you keep doing it. You need something to replace it. Paul knew that.

▶ Watch · Colossians 3:12–14then fill in the vowels
COLOSSIANS 3:12–14
Put on then, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience, bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive. And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony.

Class Notes · PUT ON column · add the missing vowels

Ask: "Why should believers forgive?" (Because the Lord has forgiven us.) "Which quality binds everything together?" (Love.) Meekness is close to humility, so the Class Notes skip it. Share your own bad-habit story here; what helped you stop?

5The swap

Back in Ephesians 4, Paul lines up opposites so you can see the trade. Every muddy thing has a clean thing that goes in its place.

▶ Watch · Ephesians 4:25–32then match the opposites
EPHESIANS 4:25–29
Having put away falsehood, let each one of you speak the truth with his neighbor… Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger… Let the thief no longer steal, but rather let him labor, doing honest work with his own hands, so that he may have something to share with anyone in need. Let no corrupting talk come out of your mouths, but only such as is good for building up…
EPHESIANS 4:31–32
Let all bitterness and wrath and anger and clamor and slander be put away from you, along with all malice. Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ forgave you.

Class Notes · match each PUT OFF to its PUT ON

Tap a muddy word, then tap its clean opposite.

New words here: bitterness = unforgiveness, holding on to old hurts; clamor = an outburst, a tantrum, the slammed door. "What do you have to do to not go to bed angry?" (Forgive them.) "Why forgive?" (Because Christ forgave us.)

6New, and being made new

Here's the part you can't skip. Being kind and forgiving does not save you. Jesus does. God makes you alive in Christ by grace, through faith. Then you get to change your clothes, every day, for the rest of your life. That has a name.

▶ Watch · sanctificationthe last piece
2 CORINTHIANS 5:17
Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come.
THE BIG WORD
Sanctification
the process of becoming more like Jesus
Make it land: we cannot make ourselves new; God does, through the Holy Spirit. Once new, we can and should put off the old and put on the righteous thoughts and actions of Jesus. Ask for other worldly things to put off and what to put on instead (video games, videos, how we talk to family this week). Suggest accountability with a parent or friend.

Colossians 2:6–7. Tap the words in order. Every right word grows the tree's roots, because that's what the verse says: rooted and built up in him.

Explain the verse first: "received Christ" = trusted him; "walk in him" = live his way every day; "rooted" = a tree that won't blow over; "built up" = a house going up a brick at a time; "abounding in thanksgiving" = overflowing with thank-yous. Play the memory verse song if you have it.
